What the results show

Diabetes and obesity

Pearson r = 0.683; Spearman rho = 0.653. Both measures indicate a positive county-level association: higher diabetes estimates generally occur alongside higher obesity estimates.

Diabetes and physical inactivity

Pearson r = 0.873; Spearman rho = 0.863. Both measures indicate a strong positive county-level association with physical inactivity estimates.

Outlier sensitivity

Cook’s distance is a screening method for points that may influence a fitted line. The commonly used 4/n threshold flagged 159 counties for diabetes and obesity, and 144 for diabetes and physical inactivity. The flagged dots remain in the charts and the main results.

When flagged counties were excluded only for a sensitivity check, Pearson correlations changed from 0.683 to 0.688 (obesity) and from 0.873 to 0.878 (physical inactivity). Spearman correlations changed from 0.653 to 0.641 and from 0.863 to 0.856, respectively. The positive relationships remained similar in direction and broad strength. A flag is a review prompt, not evidence that a data point is erroneous.

Data quality and methods

Dataset and measures

  • Public CDC PLACES county estimates, 2025 release; the three selected measures use 2023-based data.
  • Age-adjusted prevalence estimates are used consistently across measures.
  • Each dot is a county-level estimate, not a person or patient record.
  • Correlation calculations use the point estimates; they do not incorporate the estimates’ confidence intervals.

Coverage and missing estimates

  • Available coverage is 48 states plus D.C. Kentucky and Pennsylvania have no estimates for these measures based on 2023 BRFSS data in this release.
  • Loving County, Texas, has estimates suppressed by CDC because of its small population. Its values remain missing and are excluded pairwise, leaving 2,956 counties in each analysis.
  • Confidence limits are available for county estimates and appear in the county detail view when reported.

How to interpret

  • Age adjustment helps make prevalence more comparable across counties with different age distributions.
  • PLACES estimates are modeled using survey and population data; they are not direct counts of every resident.
  • County-level patterns cannot establish individual-level relationships. For example, this analysis cannot show whether a particular person with diabetes has obesity or is physically inactive.
  • Correlation does not establish causation. Other demographic, social, economic, geographic, and health factors may contribute to the observed pattern.

Uncertainty and comparison limits

  • Confidence intervals express uncertainty around each modeled estimate, but are not included in Pearson or Spearman calculations.
  • These are cross-sectional estimates for one release; they should not be treated as a trend or a ranking of overall county health.
  • State filtering updates the displayed county subset, sample count, correlations, and trend lines. The headline statistics remain the verified all-county Step 5 results.
